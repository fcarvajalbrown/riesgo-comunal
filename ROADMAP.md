Per comuna the main river is the cell inside it with the largest flow. Thresholds: 2, 5 and 20-year return levels, the GloFAS bands (yellow 2-5, red 5-20, purple 20+), fitted as GloFAS does with a Gumbel distribution by L-moments on annual maxima ([arXiv 2609.22702](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.22702)); owner asked for climate change to be accounted for, so each river's annual maxima are first tested for trend (Mann-Kendall, 5%) and, if significant, the Gumbel location is moved to the present year with Sen's slope, the time-varying-location approach of Salas and Obeysekera 2014 and Stedinger and Griffis 2011 ([FIU](https://discovery.fiu.edu/display/pub116294)); central Chile evidence conflicts (a Maule-inclusive study finds rising peaks, a 31-basin study mostly falling ones), so the direction is not assumed. Built: tools/river-points picked the main GloFAS cell in all 30 comunas from 1205 sampled cells; 14 of the 30 rivers show a significant falling trend in annual peaks (Mann-Kendall p < 0.05, Sen's slope -6 to -19 m3/s per year), the rest none, so the falling ones have lower present-year thresholds. On 10 October 2026 no comuna had any of the 51 members above its 2-year flow. On 9 October 2026 it would have shown Alto in Vichuquén (TERRA and NOAA-20 passes on one spot); ground truth unknown. NASA FIRMS added from its public 24-hour South America CSVs (VIIRS NOAA-20, NOAA-21, Suomi NPP and MODIS), which need no MAP_KEY; both Vichuquén spots were seen independently by INPE and FIRMS. Observed weather skipped for now: DMC publishes hourly SYNOP on WIS2 under the core data policy, but only as MQTT notifications (globalbroker.meteo.fr and others, topic cache/a/wis2/cl-meteochile/data/core/weather/surface-based-observations/synop) pointing to BUFR files, which needs an always-on subscriber and a BUFR decoder, so it waits for the VPS; the free aviationweather.gov METAR feed has only Curicó (SCIC, automatic, no present-weather group) inside Maule. IOC/UNESCO tide gauges added for Constitución (const) and Boyeruca (boye), per-minute data from SHOA-operated stations, non-commercial terms: new coastal-only card "Mar y tsunami ahora"; a calm day oscillates at most 0.13-0.22 m after removing the tide with a 31-minute running mean, so a gauge is flagged as anomalous (Alto) only when all its sensors pass 0.5 m in the last hour; detection lags about 15 minutes because the running mean is centred. Open-Meteo multi-model added: ECMWF IFS, GFS and ICON fetched separately (models=ecmwf_ifs025,gfs_seamless,icon_seamless); the forecast card keeps its level from the blended forecast and adds how many of the three models reach the heavy-rain threshold. Each cross-checked card (quakes, fire, forecast, sea level) shows a short "N de M fuentes" line under its headline. Layout decided by the owner (in progress): on the comuna page each hazard is one row, official alert on the left ("Sin alerta oficial" when none) and our card on the right, stacked official-first on a phone, each alert shown once (the separate rain card and the alert list go away). Alert-to-card mapping: tsunami to Tsunami and Mar y tsunami ahora; incendio to Incendio forestal and Focos de incendio; crecida, inundación and lluvia to Inundación y anegamiento; remoción en masa and aluvión also to Inundación y anegamiento for now (no second landslide source yet; NASA LHASA v2 is the only candidate found); sismo to Sismos recientes; viento, tormenta, nieve, helada, calor and general meteorological alerts to Condiciones meteorológicas; marejada and tsunami on a comuna without those cards get their own row with "Todavía no tenemos análisis propio para esta amenaza"; volcanic and unclassified alerts go in an "Otras alertas oficiales" block at the end. Rows approved by the owner: Lluvia, crecidas y aluviones (flood card); Tiempo (Condiciones meteorológicas and Pronóstico del tiempo); Incendios (Incendio forestal and Focos de incendio detectados); Sismos; Tsunami y mar (coastal, or inland only when an alert exists); Calidad del aire (in season); Otras alertas oficiales (only when any). Rows with an official alert come first, then by our level. Owner request: the tie-break order follows the season Chile is really in, checked monthly from a free climate data source (for example El Niño status against observed data) rather than a fixed calendar, because the current climate is unstable; Season-signal research: the standard wet/dry measure is the Standardized Precipitation Index (SPI, McKee et al. 1993), which the WMO recommends for meteorological drought (Lincoln Declaration 2009); it needs only precipitation, 30 years of baseline ideally, and its classes are SPI <= -1 moderately dry, -1 to 1 near normal, >= 1 wet ([droughtmanagement.info](https://droughtmanagement.info/standardized-precipitation-index-spi/), [Copernicus EDO factsheet](https://drought.emergency.copernicus.eu/data/factsheets/factsheet%5Fspi.pdf)). The DMC runs a monthly Monitor de Sequías with CIIFEN ([Meteored](https://www.meteored.cl/noticias/ciencia/monitor-de-sequia-la-nueva-herramienta-climatica-dmc-que-gana-chile.html)); a machine-readable feed was not found. For fire weather the most widely used index is the Canadian Fire Weather Index (FWI, Van Wagner 1987); CONAF rates fire danger with its own Índice de Grado de Peligro (IGP), and a Chilean study proposed the FWI instead after finding the IGP overestimated fires ([USM](https://repositorio.usm.cl/handle/11673/23481)). The 30-30-30 rule is a field warning, which CONAF says is not a strict condition ([Meteored](https://www.meteored.cl/noticias/actualidad/temporada-de-incendios-forestales-de-que-se-trata-el-factor-30-30-30-chile.html)). Built (owner chose SPI + FWI): tools/season computes both monthly from ERA5 for Talca, Curicó, Linares and Cauquenes against 1991-2020 normals; fire row first when the 30-day FWI is above normal and SPI below +1, otherwise rain first; ONI shown as context. First run, window to 9 October 2026: SPI +2.24 (extremely wet), FWI 0.6 against a normal of 2.7, so rain leads. Free inputs verified on 10 October 2026: Open-Meteo historical API (ERA5, daily precipitation since 1940, plus temperature, minimum humidity, wind, ET0 and VPD), enough to compute SPI and FWI; NOAA CPC ONI was +2.16 for Jul-Sep 2026 (strong El Niño). Page layout chosen by the owner from the list-first evidence in docs/ux.md: hazard rows full width first, the map full width below them with side gutters on a phone. Readability rules for the audience are in docs/ux.md. Next: filter persistent industrial heat sources from fire detections; heat and volcanic cards |
